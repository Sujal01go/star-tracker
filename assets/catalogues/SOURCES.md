# Catalogue sources and coordinate conventions

- `stars.json` is the app's curated bright-star subset. Where the Gaia DR3 Hipparcos crossmatch provides complete astrometry, the bundled position, parallax, and proper motion are from Gaia DR3 at its `ref_epoch` (normally J2016.0); the updater records the Gaia source ID in `source`. Gaia-incomplete and unmatched records retain their original Hipparcos / Yale values. Common names and Bayer labels are retained, with useful SIMBAD identifiers added to bright stars. `tool/update_gaia_star_catalogue.py` refreshes astrometry, and `tool/update_simbad_star_aliases.py` refreshes name cross-references. Both update the compiled offline fallback as well.
- `deep_sky.json` uses OpenNGC for NGC/IC identifications, positions, sizes, and magnitudes where present. OpenNGC is distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0. Named objects and extended features, including the approximate Cygnus Rift boundary, retain their SIMBAD provenance in each record. Coordinates use J2000.0.
- The IAU constellation list contains 88 records and keeps its traditional line connections tied to HIP identifiers in the star catalogue. `assets/constellations/iau_boundaries.json` bundles the shared official Delporte boundaries. The builder samples the B1875 boundary edges before precessing them to J2000, and the sky map draws these paths as a subtle boundary overlay. The asset is local and needs no network connection.
- JPL Horizons provides apparent geocentric coordinates for the Sun, Moon, planets, Pluto, the curated asteroids and dwarf planets, Jupiter's four Galilean moons, and 1P/Halley. The app sends requests sequentially, identifies itself with an application-specific User-Agent, caches complete snapshots in local preferences, and reduces requests after errors. The bundled analytical ephemeris remains the offline fallback; Moon and Galilean-moon Horizons positions expire sooner than those for distant bodies.
- Additional asteroid and dwarf-planet positions use JPL Small-Body Database osculating elements at the epoch stored in `planetary_ephemeris.dart`. The offline Kepler propagation omits perturbations; it is intended for sky-map finding, not precision ephemerides.
- Galilean satellite positions use JPL Horizons osculating elements for 2026-01-01 and two-body propagation. Their displayed positions are approximate.
- Meteor-shower activity dates follow the International Meteor Organization calendar. Radiant coordinates are representative J2000 positions; real radiants drift during the shower.
